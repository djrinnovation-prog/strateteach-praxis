Home-Screen Design Options

Six Directions · Analysis, Inspiration & Recommendation

Each direction is shown next to a reference from leading apps, with in-depth analysis — why it's built this way, pros and cons, and who it fits. At the end of each: links to competitor sites for thorough review.
🔒 Concept presentation only — nothing in the app was changed. Every version shows the same functions (balance/P&L, positions, daily scan, profit engine, signal bot, backtest, exchange, learn) — only arranged and styled differently.
* The "reference" screens are our own stylized renders inspired by those apps (not original screenshots), to illustrate the visual language. Links point to the official sites for independent review.
👥 This presentation goes to a panel of 8 evaluators — to choose the design direction. At the end there's a vote: choose between two at a time, until we reach the winning design.
01
Balance first
The first thing a user checks: "how much do I have / am I up?". Loss-aversion makes them look for it immediately — so it goes on top.
02
Clear primary action
What do I do now? One dominant button above the fold reduces confusion and drives usage (Fitts / Hick).
03
Thumb-reach nav
Bottom tabs = one-hand reachable, a familiar standard — zero learning curve.
04
Hierarchy & less noise
Clear groups, whitespace, and color only where attention is needed — so the eye "flows".

Cash App · simplicity

The thinking — why

This is the pattern proven by leading finance apps. The eye scans top-to-bottom by importance: balance → primary action → what's happening now (positions) → tools. The user learns nothing new — the layout is already familiar from their bank and from Revolut, and that familiarity builds trust and lowers support load. The primary action "above the fold" increases use of the core product, and the bottom nav is thumb-reachable on any screen size.

Pros
  • Familiar → instant trust
  • Zero learning curve
  • Accessible to all users
Watch out
  • Can feel "generic"
  • Less visual differentiation
Best for: broad adoption & trust — the safest default.

Key moves

  • Dominant balance card up top
  • 2 primary actions above the fold
  • Concise positions list
  • Tools as a secondary icon grid
  • 5 standard bottom tabs

Monzo · clean

The thinking — why

The goal: anyone understands what to do in a second. Distilled to one big number (the balance) and two large actions, with all "noise" removed. Per Hick's Law — fewer choices = faster decisions; a light, airy background lowers cognitive load. This is the approach that made Cash App and Monzo accessible to the masses. Excellent for onboarding and for a non-expert audience.

Pros
  • Understood in a second
  • Great onboarding
  • Broad appeal
Watch out
  • Too sparse for a power user
  • Hides depth/features
Best for: beginners & a broad audience — zero confusion.

Key moves

  • One huge balance number
  • Only 2 large action buttons
  • Light background, lots of whitespace
  • Everything else one tap away

The thinking — why

An audience coming from equities expects a certain "language": portfolio value + a performance chart up top, then holdings with percentages. A dark, restrained palette and clean typography signal "this is a real financial tool, not a toy". Robinhood made this accessible to a young crowd; Fidelity and Bloomberg keep the professional gravitas. Ideal if we want to position Strateteach as a serious investment platform.

Pros

  • Data-forward

  • Less friendly to novices
Best for: serious investors and a classic-markets feel.

Key moves

  • Performance chart under portfolio value
  • Watchlist / holdings list
  • Measured green/red percentages
  • Restrained typography, little color

The thinking — why

Crypto traders are used to a certain environment: a dark canvas, vivid green/red, a live ticker strip and fast access to action. Darkness reduces eye strain in long sessions, and vivid colors create a real-time, urgent feel. A "Today's breakouts" card up top leverages exactly what makes Strateteach unique. Binance and Bybit built this market language — adopting it makes the app feel native and natural to a trading audience.

Pros
  • Feels native to traders
  • Real-time energy
  • Highlights the breakouts
Watch out
  • Intimidating/"busy" for beginners
  • Requires familiarity
Best for: traders & the crypto space — action-focused.

Key moves

  • Dark canvas + neon accent
  • Live ticker strip up top
  • Prominent breakouts card
  • Vivid green/red for P&L

The thinking — why

For a power user who wants "everything at a glance": a widget grid — allocation, P&L, positions, breakouts, bot status — with no scrolling. High information density respects an experienced trader's time, and the layout matches the mental model of TradingView and Binance Pro.
